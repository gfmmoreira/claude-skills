# Framing by Relationship Role

Adjust tone, language, and strategic intent based on who the meeting is with. Same topic, different relationship = different conversation.

---

## Manager (your direct manager)

**What they care about:** Is this person aligned with where we're going? Are they reliable? Are there problems I need to know about early?

**What success looks like in this relationship:** Visibility into your work, trust that you'll flag issues, clarity on expectations from both sides.

**Tone:** Proactive, honest, self-aware. Show that you understand their priorities, not just your own.

**Adjustments:**
- Frame contributions in terms of impact on team or org goals, not personal effort
- When raising issues: come with context and, if possible, a proposed direction (not necessarily a solution)
- Avoid: venting without framing, bringing problems without having thought about them, positioning yourself as a victim of circumstances
- If raising something sensitive: acknowledge the difficulty, signal your intent ("I want to be upfront about something"), then say it directly
- If asking for something (feedback, promotion, resource): state the ask clearly at the start, don't bury it

**Language calibration:**
- "I wanted to flag this early rather than wait until it becomes a problem..."
- "My read on this is X — does that match what you're seeing?"
- "I'd like your perspective on how to approach this..."

---

## Peer

**What they care about:** Mutual respect, not being surprised, collaboration without games.

**What success looks like in this relationship:** Being someone they trust, go to, and don't have to manage around.

**Tone:** Direct, collegial. Less hierarchy to navigate, but also less obligation — they don't owe you feedback or support.

**Adjustments:**
- Can be more casual in framing, but don't confuse casual with vague
- Conflict topics: focus on the shared impact, not on who's right
- If you've been frustrated: lead with the effect on the work, not the personal frustration
- Avoid: escalating without trying to resolve directly first, passive language that avoids the real issue

**Language calibration:**
- "I noticed X has been creating friction between our teams — want to figure out a better setup?"
- "I wanted to run something by you before I bring it to [manager]..."
- "Honestly, here's how it landed for me..."

## Direct Report (you are their manager)

**What they care about:** Do you see their work? Do they have a future here? Are they safe to be honest with you?

**What success looks like in this relationship:** They feel heard, know where they stand, and have what they need to do good work.

**Tone:** Clear, fair, developmentally focused. You hold more power in this dynamic — use it with care.

**Adjustments:**
- Your feedback lands heavier than you think; be specific and grounded (SBI always)
- If there's a performance concern: say it plainly, early, with support — don't let it fester until review time
- Create space for their topics first, not just yours
- If they're disengaged or quiet: don't interpret it, ask about it
- Avoid: praise that's so vague it's meaningless, feedback sandwiches that bury the actual point

**Language calibration:**
- "I want to give you direct feedback on something — I'm saying this because I want you to succeed here..."
- "What's getting in your way right now?"
- "I noticed [specific thing]. What's your read on it?"

## Skip-Level (your manager's manager or senior leader)

**What they care about:** Do you think strategically? Are you someone with potential? Are there issues I should know about?

**What success looks like in this relationship:** They leave the conversation with a stronger impression of how you think and what you're working on.

**Tone:** Confident, strategic, concise. They have limited time and context — don't make them work hard to understand you.

**Adjustments:**
- Open with the headline, not the backstory
- Frame your work in terms of business outcomes, not activity
- If raising a concern: make sure it's genuinely worth their level; don't bring operational noise
- If you have a strong opinion: state it — skip-levels appreciate people who think for themselves
- Avoid: being overly deferential, rambling through context before getting to the point

**Language calibration:**
- "The most important thing I'm working on right now is X, and here's the challenge..."
- "I wanted your perspective on a direction I'm considering..."
- "I have a view on this — do you want to hear it?"

---

## Stakeholder (cross-functional, no reporting relationship)

**What they care about:** Will this person make my life easier or harder? Are they reliable? Do they understand what I need?

**What success looks like in this relationship:** Trust that you'll deliver, transparency when things change, and that you understand their constraints.

**Tone:** Collaborative, professional, explicit about dependencies. You need something from each other but have no formal authority.

**Adjustments:**
- Be explicit about what you need and by when — don't hint
- Acknowledge their constraints as real, not just obstacles
- If something has gone wrong: acknowledge it briefly, redirect to what's changing
- Avoid: over-committing to manage the relationship, vagueness about timelines or scope

**Language calibration:**
- "I want to make sure we're aligned on what's coming from my side and when..."
- "What would make this work better from your end?"
- "I need to flag a dependency that affects your timeline..."
